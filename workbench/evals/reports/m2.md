## What it shows

Semantic search alone finds a quarter of the labelled properties in its top 8. It does well when the question uses the same words as the listings: "cosy flat with a garden" finds 8 of 8. It finds nothing when the question paraphrases the term the listings use, such as "separate annexe" for a granny flat, "a bit of land" for land, "nobody has lived in before" for a new build, or "renovation project" for a development opportunity. A small local embedding model does not bridge those gaps, which is the case for adding keyword search beside it.

Search is fast enough to sit inside a chat turn: 65 ms at p95 with the model and index warm. The index held every MySQL row exactly once.

## How it is scored

Questions come from `workbench/evals/golden-set.json`, picked by the milestones and slices above, each labelled by a rule over the generated fields as `workbench/evals/README.md` describes. A question's recall at k is the relevant properties found in its top k, divided by k or by the number of relevant properties, whichever is smaller, so a perfect top k scores 100 percent. Uncertain properties, which the record points to but cannot prove, are taken out of the results before the cut: finding one neither earns a hit nor uses up a slot. The API returns at most 20, so a question with an uncertain property in its top 20 is judged on fewer at 20.

Latency is the round trip of `POST /search/semantic` inside the process, after every question has been asked once, so the model and the index are warm. The questions are asked in turn until the sample is full.

The index check lists every document id in the index and every property id in MySQL. A property with no document is missing; a document with no property, or an id held twice, is extra.
